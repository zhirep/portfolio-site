FireWatch

Project Summary

FireWatch was our final project for the Web Development course. FireWatch is a web application that visualizes wildfire activity across Alberta by plotting them in an interactive map and display their status. Additionally, weather is also displayed.

FireWatch also provides its users with valuable information such as wildfire prevention and what to do if one were to happen. It also has optional features such as login, signup, as well as contacts functionalities.

Problem and Goal

Wildfires get common in Alberta especially around the summer season. With FireWatch, you are able to keep track of their location and status in a more accesible and intuitive way.

My Role

My primary role in this project was database management. I implemented database functionality that supports login, signup, and contact which lets users send us messages if they happen to have any question or inquiries. I used the database called MongoDB, where I made different collections for different purposes, mainly for login and contact information.

I also implemented front end pages, specifically the About Us page which shows the users who we are and what we do, our mission, and values. For the About Us page, I did research on similar websites and based it off of it, mainly for their visuals and layout of the page.

Video Transcript

  • The user proceeds to sign up for an account. The user inputs their email, password, username, first/last name, address, city, province, and postal code.
  • Upon completion, the user is redirected to the login page, where they can sign in using the account they just created.
  • The user verifies in MongoDB that the account is made and saved in the collection.
  • The Homepage is the main chunk of the website. Here, the user can interact with the map. Wildfire status is plotted and marked on the map. Clicking on a point reveals the wildfire status. The project was made during winter, so at the time, there were no active wildfires. Moreover, weather blobs are also on the map, even outside of Alberta.
  • The user may also view charts of information about current and past wildfire details, such as their status, cause of fire, fire size distribution, and more.
  • Additionally, there are pages that explain about the team's mission and values, as well as an education page that explains users prevention and what to do during a wildfire.
  • The website also has a dedicated Contact page, where the user, regardless of whether logged in or not, can send the team messages/inquiries that they may have.
  • Lastly, the user logs out of their account.
