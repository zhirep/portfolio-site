Virtual Vials

Project Summary

This project initially was a chemistry experiment simulation, but as we went deeper into the research and planning phase, we decided to change the scope of the project into a visualization of molecules instead. More details of the initial project are included below in the Design Document v1 file. Virtual Vials was the project my team and I made for our capstone project. Virtual Vials will primarily focus on developing a VR-based learning environment for Newman Projections. It will involve users simulating a laboratory setting to enable interactive visualization of molecular structures.

Problem and Goal

Nowadays, when learning something new, motivation and sometimes even the cost often become significant barriers to learning. Motivation is dependent on students; some may struggle to stay motivated over time especially without any clear goal or rewards. Fortunately, according to some research, blending learning and engagement tools such as virtual reality increases productivity of students as it supplements traditional learning with "gamified" methods. With Virtual Vials, students are able to access and participate in a "real" lab envionment without running into any risk or high costs, regardless of their experience level or knowledge. As opposed to just reading, this gives them the opportunity to bridge the gap between reading materials and hands-on experience.

My Role

Before any development was done, I did some research on what makes virtual learning good, as opposed to just reading them. I learned that VR can be a good supplement for learning as it can provide real-time feedback based on the maipulation and interaction of objects, in our case, molecules in the simulation. This encourages trial and error learning and deeper engagement to the material, which in turn, boosts the confidence and motivation of students.

My role in the project focused primarily on the game's visuals, which included all the UI elements, assets, and scenes. Additionally, I implemented gameplay features for all VR interactions and SFX to ensure good user flow. I also worked on one of the core features of the app, which was the spawning of the atoms. I worked with the team to ensure the spawned atoms were accurate and functioned as intended. I also worked on the early version of the Builder functionality, which was a completely different version than what we have now as it involved combining molecule templates with VR socket and grab interactions.

Another major role I did was conducting user tests. I conducted survey questions that focused on asking users their opinions about the app in general such as the controls, comfortability using the app, comments on what should be added or changed, and whether or not it was helpful.

User Testing Results

We let 11 students playtest our app and usability seemed to be an overall success, as the general consensus leaned towards the higher end. However, VR comfort was a mixed bag as a few students experienced discomfort, which is to be expected given that it was their first experience with VR.

Video Transcript

  • The application loads the user in the menu room, where they can start the game, toggle the tutorial area, adjust volume settings, or quit the app.
  • The tutorial room walks the user through general VR controls, as well as teaches them the mechanics of the app, such as snapping objects together, opening the molecule bank, and building and deleting molecules.
  • The hub room provides users with information about the game and its two rooms.
  • The visual room provides users with information about the molecule template. In here, they can spawn atoms to build the molecules with. Upon completion, it shows the user its Newman projection.
  • The builder room provides users about Newman projections. It also lets them build any molecule and dynamically displays its corresponding Newman projection when selecting a bond.
